I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Sciences at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Bilge Mutlu. I'm on the 2026–27 faculty job market.

My research spans human-computer interaction, artificial intelligence, and robotics, turning people's often-implicit expectations into shared representations that people can shape and AI agents can act on. These expectations matter because, as agents increasingly act on people's behalf, carrying out a request well requires accounting not only for what people explicitly ask but also for what they may never think to state. They are difficult to address because people often take them for granted and cannot articulate them all in advance, while agents cannot reliably infer what remains unstated. I call this challenge the gulf of delegation, and I build systems and interaction mechanisms that help people specify what should guide an agent, check how that guidance is used, and revise what carries forward.

In 2026, I worked at Amazon with the Fauna Robotics team on character-based memory for household robots.

My research is supported by the Cisco Distinguished Graduate Fellowship and the NSF INTEGRATE Research Traineeship.
